Private LLM Inference Decision Matrix: Moat vs. Myth

A scoring framework that weighs latency tolerance, privacy needs, scale, and budget against the four real deployment options. Score your use case 1–5 per axis; the matrix points at the quadrant winner — and prioritizes near-term viability over crypto fantasy.

Executive Summary Roughly 90% of enterprise "private LLM" requirements are met today by self-hosted or TEE-based inference at approximately 1/10th the cost and latency of fully cryptographic alternatives. Pure FHE and MPC remain demonstration-grade in 2026 — published benchmarks show slowdowns ranging from 10× (hybrid MPC) to over 1,000,000× (pure FHE) versus cleartext baselines. The matrix below scores a use case across five criteria; the resulting score routes the buyer to one of three quadrants with a defensible, cited rationale.

Usage Guide

  1. Score your scenario 1–5 per axis. Example: agentic workflow → latency=5, privacy=3, scale=4, moat=4, budget=4.
  2. Identify the winning quadrant. Self-hosted or TEE wins ~90% of cases. Defer pure FHE unless a quantum-threat or nation-state-actor model is in scope.
  3. ROI calc. Self-host breaks even in 3–6 months at 10M tokens/month vs. priced APIs.[6]
  4. Risk priorities. Model theft > data leaks. Focus TEE attestation + watermarking before considering fully encrypted inference.[9]
  5. Pitch line: "TEE deployment, breakeven in 3 months on our volume, sovereignty moat via attested compute, no cryptographic theater."

Why this matters for Airlock

The Airlock substrate routes ~90% of behavioral traffic to budget mixture-of-experts models running on commodity self-hosted or TEE-attested hardware (per ConstellationBench 0.1), and reserves frontier dense models for the ~2% of reasoning-critical traffic. The matrix above is the buyer-facing version of the same decision: most "private LLM" buyers are actually buying TEE or self-host, and the cryptographic alternatives are research-stage. Our pricing model — 10% activation + 20% of savings + a referral program — captures margin on the savings the customer realizes by not over-buying cryptographic privacy they cannot use.

Limits of this matrix

The numbers above are 2026 production benchmarks for general-purpose LLM inference. Specialized vertical workloads (small models, batched inference, narrow domains) may shift FHE/MPC into viable territory earlier. The matrix is a starting point for the conversation, not a final answer. Re-score every six months; revisit when H200 / B300 confidential-compute SKUs land or when a published FHE benchmark crosses the 5× cleartext-overhead threshold.

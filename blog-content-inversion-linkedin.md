# The Great Content Inversion (LinkedIn version)

*By Zachary Holwerda · April 30, 2026*

---

In November 2024, daily AI-generated articles surpassed human-written ones for the first time in recorded history. By end of 2026, projections put human-authored content below 10% of the public web.

This is the **Great Content Inversion** — and the economics underneath it explain why your next model might be dumber than the last one.

**Three things break at once:**

**1. Model collapse.** When AI trains on AI, fidelity degrades. The first casualty is rare events — minority perspectives, regional dialects, edge cases. The collapse is silent.

**2. Scarcity.** High-quality human text data exhausts in 2026. Reddit licensing deals, Stack Overflow agreements, NYT lawsuits — these are not corporate squabbles. They are the visible edge of a structural resource event.

**3. Cost.** Reasoning-token demand grew **320× in 2025**. A reasoning query consumes 50–100× the energy of a standard completion. AI is projected to use 22% of US electricity by 2028.

**The defense is not a policy document. It is a runtime.**

- **Positive friction** — time delays, manual confirmations, and explicit "why do you agree" prompts that interrupt cognitive drift (healthy human–AI agreement is 80–95%; above 95% you are rubber-stamping, not reviewing).
- **V3 Governance** — Truth Config + Evidence Pack + DID-signed promotion + Runtime gate. Agents propose. Humans authorize. Shadow AI becomes architecturally impossible.
- **Provenance Filter** — six rules at the public/audited boundary. Missing provenance is a hard protocol error, not a warning.
- **Untangler Gates** — anomaly detection in the compute layer itself, not bolted on after generation. Anomalous closures are blocked inline.

In an ocean of AI Slop, authentic human experience becomes the most valuable and scarce resource in the economy.

The most valuable contribution you can make is not faster prompting. It is **slower verification**.

AI proposes. Humans authorize. That is not a stylistic preference. It is a structural requirement.

Why they lose

It's not the picks. It's the heart.

The losses aren't a knowledge problem — they're an emotion problem. The research is blunt about it:

  • 3 of 4 bettors placed an impulse bet last year — and 1 in 7 make every bet on impulse.
  • 60% admit to chasing losses — betting bigger to win it back.
  • Fans bet their own team regardless of the numbers — emotional attachment predicts the bet.
  • "Tilting" after a bad beat lines up with the highest problem-gambling severity.
Every one of these is the gap between what a bettor feels and what the numbers say. That gap is exactly where the Fixer lives — the honest number, and a nudge to sit down when your heart's running hot.

Why it works

Our edge isn't being smarter.
It's being honest about what hits.

Most models brag. They'll call a game 70% and hit 59%. Ours does the same raw — so we calibrate every number down to what actually lands across thousands of graded picks. The percent you see is the percent that hits.

Then we compare that honest number to the live, de-vigged market. The gap is the edge. When the model screams a huge gap, we don't chase it — we fade our own outlier, because a gap that wide usually means the model's missing something the market already knows.

What it costs to run

The margin is in owning the voice.

At 1,000 subscribers × $39.99, that's about $40,000/month in. The main cost isn't idle servers — it's the voice itself (Otto talking), which scales with minutes used, not users signed up. So the whole game is rent the voice, or own it:

~70% margin

This is why the hardware is in the budget, not the wish-list: bringing the voice in-house is what turns a thin business into a real one as it grows.

Estimates, not promises — real numbers move with usage and pricing. Text inference (the model reading the game) is cheap; voice is the lever. Adding the other real-time engines (the SSM stack) rides the same owned hardware.

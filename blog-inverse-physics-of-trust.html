1. Read the voids

Most physics is taught as a study of what is there. Forces act on bodies. Fields propagate through space. Particles interact and you write down the equation. This works, but it leaves a hidden half of the discipline unsaid: the structure of what is not there is just as load-bearing as the structure of what is.

A solid is defined by its boundary, but the boundary is a void. A wave is defined by its trough as much as its crest. A sentence is shaped by what the speaker chose not to say. A market is priced by the bid that nobody made. The signal you can read is held in shape by the silence around it.

This is what we mean by inverse physics. Read the voids. The substrate fills the gap between what is said and what isn't, and the fill — not the source data — is where trust lives.

This essay closes the patent series. The five preceding posts each introduced one of the five patents in the Airlock portfolio. Each patent is the operational form of an underlying constant. The constants are older than the patents. The patents are how we make those constants do useful work in a probabilistic substrate.

The substrate beneath the model is what closes the loop. The substrate is deterministic at the boundary even when the model is probabilistic in the middle. 8. What you can do with this

If you are designing a system that has to produce trust under probabilistic conditions, the inverse-physics frame gives you a checklist:

  1. Find the void. Where is the loop currently open? What absence is your model failing to read?
  2. Shape it. The gap has to have a boundary the substrate can walk. A kōan only works if the contradiction is sharp.
  3. Close it deterministically. The fill must come from a signed authority — a DID, a verifiable credential, a deterministic replay, a cooperative ledger entry. Probabilistic fills are not closures.
  4. Audit the closure. The closed loop is a trust unit only if you can replay it. Every BCODE measurement leaves an audit row.
  5. Stack the closures. One closed loop is a moment of trust. A stack of closed loops is the substrate.

That is the five-step ladder, restated in operator vocabulary. The substrate fills the void; the void was shaped by the surrounding context; the fill is signed; the signature is auditable; the audit is the moat.
